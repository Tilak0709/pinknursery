Snake Plant

About

A quiet, architectural companion

A hardy, low-maintenance indoor plant with tall, upright, sword-like leaves. It brings clean, architectural lines to homes, offices and any corner that needs a touch of green, and it is happy to be left alone.

Benefits

Why you’ll love it

  1. A modern, elegant look

    Its strong vertical form suits contemporary interiors, from a living-room corner to a reception desk.

  2. Copes with low light

    It grows best in bright, indirect light, but adapts to dimmer rooms and offices.

  3. Breathes at night

    It uses CAM photosynthesis: its leaf pores open in the cool of the night to take in carbon dioxide, which helps it save water during the day.

  4. Made for busy lives

    It thrives on a little neglect, so a missed watering is easily forgiven.

Maintenance

How to keep it thriving

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light is ideal. It tolerates lower light but grows more slowly there. Gentle morning sun is fine; harsh afternoon sun through glass can scorch the leaves.

  • Water

    Usually every 2–3 weeks, but only once the soil has dried out completely. In the monsoon and cooler months, about once a month is enough. Pour around the base, not into the centre of the leaves.

  • Soil

    A well-draining potting mix, such as a cactus or succulent mix, in a pot with a drainage hole.

  • Temperature

    Comfortable between 15 and 30 °C. Keep it away from cold air-conditioner drafts.

  • Feeding

    Optional. A balanced liquid fertiliser at half strength, once a month in spring and summer. Skip it in the cooler months.

  • Repotting

    Every 2–3 years, or when roots crowd the pot. It likes a snug fit, so go up just one pot size.

The golden rule

When in doubt, don’t water.

Overwatering is the most common way to lose a snake plant, because soggy soil causes root rot. Push a finger a few centimetres into the soil: if it is dry all the way down, water thoroughly and let it drain. If not, wait.

Troubleshooting

Reading the leaves

Soft, yellow or mushy leaves
Too much water. Let the soil dry out completely and check the roots for rot.
Wrinkled or curling leaves
Thirsty. Give it a thorough drink, then let the excess drain away.
Brown, crispy tips
Uneven watering. Settle into a steady routine.
Pale, stretched or leaning leaves
Not enough light. Move it closer to a bright window.

Pets & little ones

Snake plants are mildly toxic if chewed and can upset the stomach of cats, dogs and small children. Place it where curious mouths can’t reach.

A gentle nudge

Never miss a check-in

Add a reminder every 14 days to check the soil. Your calendar will tell you when; the soil will tell you whether to water.
