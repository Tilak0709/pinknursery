About this guide

A living part of the workplace

Plants bring natural colour, texture and a sense of calm to shared spaces. This guide introduces the varieties selected for the Porter office and offers practical starting points for their care.

A note on care

Watering needs vary with pot size, light, air-conditioning, season and drainage. Check the potting mix before watering rather than following a rigid calendar. The guidance here is a general indoor-care guide; The Pink Nursery maintenance team adjusts it to the actual conditions in the office.

Snake Plant

Dracaena trifasciata

Also known as Mother-in-law’s tongue

A hardy, architectural foliage plant with upright, sword-shaped leaves. Its neat growth habit makes it a popular choice for contemporary offices and indoor spaces.

Light
Low to bright indirect
Water
When fully dry
Native to
Tropical West Africa

Care guide

  • Light

    Low to bright indirect light; growth is usually better in brighter indirect light.

  • Water

    Allow the potting mix to dry out fully or almost fully before watering. Avoid leaving the pot standing in water.

  • Care

    Use a free-draining potting mix. Remove dry leaves at the base and wipe dust from the leaves. Overwatering is a common cause of problems.

Good to know

It uses CAM photosynthesis: its leaf pores open at night to take in carbon dioxide, which helps it save water.

Bamboo Palm

Chamaedorea seifrizii

Also known as Reed palm

A graceful, clumping palm with slender stems and soft, feathery fronds. It brings a relaxed, tropical feel to indoor corners and workspaces.

Light
Bright, filtered
Water
When the top dries
Native to
Mexico and Central America

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, filtered or indirect light; protect from harsh direct afternoon sun.

  • Water

    Water when the upper layer of the potting mix begins to dry. Keep lightly and evenly moist, never waterlogged.

  • Care

    Provide good drainage and occasional leaf cleaning. Trim only fully brown or dry fronds near the base.

Good to know

Its slender, cane-like stems resemble bamboo, which is how it got its common name.

China Doll

Radermachera sinica

Also known as Emerald tree, serpent tree

A compact ornamental tree with delicate, glossy leaflets and a fine-textured canopy. Its lush foliage gives indoor spaces a polished, leafy look.

Light
Bright, indirect
Water
When the top dries
Native to
Southern China and Taiwan

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light is best. Avoid sudden changes in position and strong direct sun.

  • Water

    Water when the top layer of the potting mix starts to dry. Do not let the plant sit in excess water.

  • Care

    Keep conditions consistent and avoid frequent moving. Prune lightly to maintain shape and remove dry or damaged leaves.

Good to know

In the wild it grows into a large tree; indoors it stays compact and shrub-like.

Money Plant

Epipremnum aureum

Also known as Golden pothos, devil’s ivy

A versatile climbing or trailing foliage plant with attractive green, often golden-variegated leaves. It works well on shelves, partitions and in planters with room for its vines.

Light
Medium to bright indirect
Water
When the top few cm are dry
Native to
Mo’orea, French Polynesia

Care guide

  • Light

    Medium to bright indirect light. Variegated forms generally need brighter indirect light to retain their pattern.

  • Water

    Water when the top few centimetres of potting mix feel dry. Empty excess water from the saucer or planter.

  • Care

    Trim long vines to encourage fuller growth and remove yellow leaves. Provide a support if it is being trained to climb.

Good to know

It is nicknamed devil’s ivy because it is so hard to kill and stays green even in low light.

Areca Palm

Dypsis lutescens

Also known as Butterfly palm, golden cane palm

A popular clumping palm with arching, feathery fronds. Its soft silhouette adds height, movement and a welcoming tropical character to an office.

Light
Bright, indirect
Water
When the top dries
Native to
Madagascar

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light is ideal. Some gentle morning sun may be tolerated after gradual acclimatisation.

  • Water

    Water when the top layer of soil begins to dry, keeping the mix lightly moist but not soggy.

  • Care

    Ensure good drainage, wipe dust from fronds and remove fully brown leaves. Avoid prolonged dry conditions and cold drafts.

Good to know

Its stems turn golden-yellow as they mature, which is why it is also called the golden cane palm.

Bird of Paradise

Strelitzia species

A striking statement plant recognised for its large, paddle-shaped leaves. It creates a bold, sculptural focal point in spacious office areas.

Light
Bright, some gentle sun
Water
When the upper soil dries
Native to
Southern Africa

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright light is important; a position with some gentle direct sunlight can support strong growth.

  • Water

    Water thoroughly when the upper portion of the potting mix has started to dry. Reduce watering in cooler, lower-light periods.

  • Care

    Give leaves room to spread, wipe them gently and remove old or damaged leaves. Rotate the pot occasionally for balanced growth.

Good to know

It is named after its flowers, which look like the head of a brightly coloured bird.

Song of India

Dracaena reflexa

Also known as Pleomele

A decorative dracaena with narrow, often yellow-edged or variegated leaves arranged along branching stems. It adds colour, structure and vertical interest.

Light
Bright, indirect
Water
When the upper layer dries
Native to
Madagascar and the Indian Ocean islands

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light helps maintain leaf colour. Avoid harsh direct sunlight.

  • Water

    Allow the upper layer of potting mix to dry before watering. Avoid constantly wet soil.

  • Care

    Remove dry leaves and prune tips or stems to maintain the desired shape. Use well-draining soil and avoid waterlogging.

Good to know

Its species name, reflexa, means “bent back”, after the way its leaves curve.

Rubber Plant

Ficus elastica

Also known as Rubber fig, Indian rubber tree

A bold foliage plant with large, thick, glossy leaves. Its strong upright form makes it an elegant feature plant for office interiors.

Light
Bright, indirect
Water
When the top few cm are dry
Native to
Northeast India and Southeast Asia

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light. Acclimatise gradually if moving it to a brighter position.

  • Water

    Water when the top few centimetres of soil are dry. Reduce frequency when light levels or temperatures are lower.

  • Care

    Wipe leaves to remove dust and maintain their natural sheen. Remove damaged leaves and provide a stable pot with good drainage.

Good to know

It grows wild in the forests of northeast India, and its milky sap was once used to make rubber.

Dwarf Umbrella

Schefflera arboricola

Also known as Dwarf umbrella tree

A compact foliage shrub with leaflets arranged like the spokes of an umbrella. It is useful for adding a full, leafy form to indoor settings.

Light
Bright to moderate
Water
When the top dries
Native to
Taiwan and Hainan

Care guide

  • Light

    Bright, indirect light; it can tolerate moderate light, though growth may be slower.

  • Water

    Water when the top layer of potting mix dries. Do not keep the roots continuously wet.

  • Care

    Pinch or prune growing tips to maintain a compact shape. Remove dead foliage and check periodically for common indoor plant pests.

Good to know

Each leaf is made of several leaflets radiating from one point, just like the ribs of an umbrella.
